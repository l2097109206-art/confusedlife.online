The Clarity Quiz is a reflective self-check. Specifically:

  • It has not been clinically validated and is not a screening or diagnostic instrument for any condition.
  • A score is a snapshot of one week, not a judgement about you or your life.
  • It runs entirely in your browser. No answers are transmitted, stored, or associated with you in any way.
  • It is not a substitute for assessment by a professional. If your result concerns you, that's worth raising with a GP or therapist — not with a website.

When to seek professional help

Ordinary confusion responds to rest, honest reflection and small changes. The following are worth taking to a GP, therapist or counsellor rather than working through alone:

  • Low mood, emptiness or hopelessness lasting most days for more than two weeks.
  • Loss of interest or pleasure in things that used to reliably matter to you.
  • Significant, sustained changes to sleep or appetite.
  • Difficulty functioning at work, in study, or in relationships you care about.
  • Drinking or using substances more than usual to manage how you feel.
  • Thinking about harming yourself, or feeling that people would be better off without you.

That last one deserves emphasis. If you're having thoughts of suicide, please contact a crisis line now — 988 in the US, 116 123 in the UK, or findahelpline.com elsewhere. Crisis lines are not only for people at immediate risk, and the people answering are not shocked by the call.
