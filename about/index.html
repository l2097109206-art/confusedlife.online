About

Who we are

ConfusedLife.online is an independent editorial project publishing plain-English guides for people who feel lost, stuck or confused about life. No course, no coaching funnel, no 5am routine waiting at the end of an article.

Why this site exists

Most content about feeling lost falls into two camps, and neither is much use.

The first is the inspirational kind: quotes on sunsets, assertions that everything happens for a reason, and advice to simply follow your passion. It's pleasant to read and changes nothing, because it assumes the hard part is motivation.

The second is the clinical kind: accurate, careful, and written in the register of a medical textbook, which means it's genuinely useful to someone researching a diagnosis and much less useful to someone lying awake at 1am wondering whether they've wasted their twenties.

We're trying to write the thing in between — honest about what the research does and doesn't support, and written in the language people actually use when they talk about this in private.

What we do

  • We research before we write. Every guide draws on published psychology and, where we make a claim about how something works, we link to the source. Where the evidence is thin — and on questions of meaning, it often is — we say so rather than dressing up an opinion as science.
  • We don't sell a transformation. There's nothing to buy here. The advice is deliberately cheap, boring and unglamorous, because that's the kind that survives contact with an ordinary Wednesday.
  • We update rather than republish. Guides carry a visible "updated" date and get revised when we learn something better, rather than being quietly rewritten to chase a keyword.
  • We name our limits. We're not clinicians, and we say so on every relevant page. Where a question is medical, we point to people qualified to answer it.

Who writes it

Content is produced by the ConfusedLife editorial team — a small group of writers and researchers with backgrounds in psychology, journalism and education. We publish under a team byline rather than inventing individual experts, because we'd rather be transparent about that than manufacture authority.

We are not licensed mental health professionals. Nothing on this site is medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. When a guide touches on something that genuinely warrants professional input, we say so in the guide itself — not just in a disclaimer nobody reads.

The full detail is in our editorial policy.

How the site is funded

Currently it isn't, beyond the cost of the domain and hosting. We may add advertising in future; if we do, it will be clearly labelled, it won't influence what we publish, and the privacy policy will be updated to reflect it before anything goes live.

We don't run affiliate links to courses, coaching programmes or supplements, and we have no plans to. The conflict of interest is too direct on a topic like this.

Get in touch

Corrections are genuinely welcome — if we've got something wrong, mischaracterised a study, or stated something more confidently than the evidence supports, please tell us. We'd rather fix it than be right.

We can't offer personal advice or respond to individual situations, not because we don't want to but because we're not qualified to. If you need someone to talk to, the disclaimer page lists crisis and support resources by country.
